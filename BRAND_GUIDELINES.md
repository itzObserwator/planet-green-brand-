# Planet Green · Identity guidelines

**Version 0.2 — original logo integrated; application study, 18 November 2026.**

## Purpose and voice

The identity follows Planet Green’s published vision: help people reduce the pressure on nature so it can recover. Its mission reconnects people with preparing food, growing produce and replenishing soil. Vinay Ram’s account links his experience in natural farming with the idea of combining farming and everyday living in communities.

Use the three invitations consistently: **Indulge** in nature, **Involve** yourself in growing food and caring for land, and **Impact** nature through thoughtful care. “Move to Nature” remains the brand’s invitation. Write with warmth and practical verbs such as grow, learn, care and replenish. Keep regeneration framed as the ambition, without asserting measured outcomes, certifications or guaranteed health benefits.

The vision and mission shown in this study are paraphrases, not attributed quotations. The official About page identifies Vinay Ram as Managing Director. Sources checked on 19 November 2026: [About Planet Green](https://planetgreen.co.in/about-us.html) and [Philosophy](https://planetgreen.co.in/philosophy.html).

### Story architecture

The organising idea is **what sustains us deserves our care**. A closer life begins with attention and deepens through participation. Lead with an existing relationship—the food we eat, the soil that grows it, the places we inhabit—then offer practical ways to experience it. Planet Green’s vision is an organisational commitment to nature’s recovery. It does not assign the reader a predetermined purpose in life. The mission supplies concrete practices: make food, grow produce and replenish soil.

The user’s philosophical reference is the distinction between searching for an assigned life purpose and cultivating the capacity to experience life consciously. Relevant primary references: [Sadhguru on the purpose of life](https://isha.sadhguru.org/en/wisdom/video/what-is-the-purpose-of-life) and [Isha’s description of Inner Engineering](https://isha.sadhguru.org/yoga/yoga-programs/inner-engineering/). The Planet Green copy applies the broad ideas of attention, direct experience and practical participation in its own voice. It does not reproduce Sadhguru’s manner of speaking, offer yogic methods, claim spiritual effects or imply affiliation. The practical bridge is to Planet Green’s own work with food, farming, living and land.

Sarvam’s [brand case study](https://www.sarvam.ai/brand) remains a reference for narrative coherence: the central idea explains the visual choices and carries into applications. The user’s Katha Darpan image references now inform the homepage presentation: textured paper, architectural framing, restrained ornament and spacious cultural-story chapters. The original signature, photographic story, reusable components and interaction patterns carry into that setting.

### Rewritten narrative

**Move to Nature. What sustains us deserves our care.**

A meal begins long before it reaches the table. It begins with soil, a seed, a season and someone’s care. Our lives are already part of that relationship. Planet Green invites us to experience it more closely, bringing cultivation, nourishment and everyday living into the same story.

**An Indian story of earth and nourishment.** In Hindu tradition, Bhudevi personifies earth. Annapurna is associated with nourishment, depicted offering food to Shiva. These references bring earth and food into human relationship. Planet Green’s contemporary interpretation carries that relationship into everyday acts: growing, preparing, sharing and replenishing.

**The distance we can close.** Food can reach us without the field ever entering our thoughts. A home can feel separate from the landscape around it. Planet Green brings those relationships back into view, giving people practical ways to encounter what sustains them.

**A lived encounter gives the idea a place to begin.** Vinay Ram’s work in real estate took a new direction when he began learning natural farming. Working with the soil brought farming and living into the same frame. Planet Green grew from that connection: places where people could enjoy nature, participate in cultivation and learn through their own involvement. His published account supplies this factual foundation; the mythological reading is the case study’s editorial interpretation, not a quotation or an origin story attributed to him.

**A commitment expressed through practice.** Planet Green’s vision is to ease the burden on Mother Nature so she has room to recover. Its mission brings that commitment into things people can do: make food, grow produce and replenish soil. Prepare a meal with attention to its ingredients. Grow something and learn the conditions it needs. Consider what we return to the ground that supports the next harvest.

**Four expressions of the same relationship.**

- **Farm Natura — begin where nourishment grows.** Natural farming and farmland living make the relationship tangible through cultivation. Care has a practical expression in what we grow and what we return to the soil.
- **The Eco World — carry the relationship into how we live.** Home, landscape and community belong in the same conversation. Attention to trees, contours and shared green spaces keeps the land part of everyday surroundings.
- **Veda Bharath — meet the story at the table.** Traditional Indian rice varieties and natural farming connect a familiar grain with the knowledge and care behind it. Nourishment offers an everyday way to remember the land.
- **Green Land Capital (GLC) — make a starting point navigable.** Its buyer, landowner and authorised-partner network provides a route through farmland verification and transactions. Access begins an encounter; land use and care carry the relationship forward.

**Knowing grows through doing.** An idea gains depth through experience. Indulge, Involve and Impact offer room to begin with enjoyment, become involved and consider the effects of our choices. Each person can choose a point of entry. The story offers a relationship to explore; the organisation’s vision guides its work.

**A story lived together.** Begin at the table, in cultivation, in the landscape of everyday living or with a route to farmland. Curiosity opens the relationship. Participation deepens it. Care gives it continuity. What sustains us deserves our care: learn the relationship, take part in it, and give something back to the ground.

### Integrating supplied artwork

1. Place approved originals in `public/brand/` without altering their geometry.
2. Set the corresponding `brandAssets` paths in `src/lib/brand-tokens.ts`. Use `primary` for original SVG and `primaryPng` for approved PNG. Configure monochrome/symbol only if separately supplied and approved.
3. Adjust intrinsic dimensions in `BrandLogo` to match the artwork's actual aspect ratio. Preserve its natural colours in all previews.
4. Validate contrast on the dark and light preview panels. Mark any unsupported treatment pending approval.
5. Establish clear-space and minimum display sizes from the original art and brand owner's instructions. Update the guide and this document together.
6. Create logo-derived repeat patterns only from approved vector geometry and only with approval.

## Visual language

Nature-forward, modern and premium, organic and fluid, human-centred, layered and immersive. Use editorial spacing, real photography, quiet borders and depth with purpose. Avoid folk-art, hand-drawn illustrations, unrelated accent colours, neon effects and constant motion.

Generic flow shapes, contour lines, gradients and halftone in this repository are original concept studies, not geometry derived from the Planet Green logo. Do not present them as official brand assets. The logo-derived pattern slot remains pending.

### Hills, soil and life

Express the vision through landscape imagery, close photographs of soil and plant life, and clear typography. Do not use drawn landscape cutaways, root diagrams, illustrated nature motifs or cartoon elements. Keep the original logo and functional icons intact. Nature imagery expresses the ambition and must not be presented as evidence of measured environmental outcomes.

## Surfaces and motion

Use the restored soft glass material over imagery. It uses 8 px native backdrop blur, a translucent green tint and a fine edge. A softly blurred enlarged crop of the assigned scene provides the previous visual effect. Keep the current 38 px feature-card radius and existing card dimensions, padding and alignment. This is a visual glass treatment, not a physical optical simulation. Text and original logos remain unfiltered. Documentation dialogs retain a light reading surface; reduced-transparency and forced-colour modes use accessible fallbacks.

Interface transitions use 240 ms and `cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1)`. Hero pointer parallax is subtle and desktop-only in practice (mouse pointers). The opt-in 3D study rotates slowly. Respect reduced motion; avoid scroll hijacking. Do not animate essential information out of reach.

## Icons

Use Lucide React with round caps/joins. Standard sizes: 16, 24, 32 px. Default stroke: 1.5 px; 2 px is available for denser contexts. Inherit shared token colours. Prefer outlined icons; do not fill arbitrary icons. Pair unfamiliar symbols with text and give standalone controls accessible names. These are interface icons, never a replacement for the original globe logo.

## Imagery

Prioritise natural light, real materials, restrained colour, believable farming and human interaction. Categories: macro, landscape, soil/growth, people/nature, architecture/community and natural textures. Use rounded masks, subtle environmental overlays, optional halftone or soft focus. Avoid oversaturation. People-led imagery still needs commissioning; the garden reference is labelled as a placeholder.

The hero render was generated for this study. Photography selections are Unsplash references, not approved Planet Green campaign assets. Verify production rights and any model/property releases for the final use. See the provenance file for exact source URLs and the generation prompt.

## The Living Cycle / The Act of Giving Back

**What nature gives, we return through care.** Receive introduces nature’s
canopy; Realise connects light with flowing botanical material; Restore places a
small seedling in an open supporting hand. The approved master A combines these
relationships into one asymmetrical composition. This is contemporary illustration,
not a historical or sacred motif, and it supports the unchanged original logo.

Use the complete master for large campaigns and hero accents. Use a companion for
smaller editorial cards. Fine veins and hand anatomy need space: do not reduce the
master to a tiny interface icon. Keep typography outside its detailed regions.
Transparent assets work on white or Forest; use the library preview to check edges
and tonal detail. Do not recolour the artwork with CSS filters.

Animate whole separate images with restrained transforms or opacity. Do not
simulate independently moving fingers, leaf veins or water inside a flattened
image. The story sequence has manual selection, pause and replay, stops outside
the viewport, and disables autoplay under reduced motion. Existing engravings
remain available for stories of seed, soil, hills and life.

Reusable components live in `src/components/giving-back/GivingBackArtwork.tsx`.
`BotanicalAccent` reuses Receive and `SupportingHandIllustration` reuses Restore,
preventing visual drift. The **Giving back** view in the component library includes
seven usage examples, PNG/WebP downloads, a five-part inspector and application
previews. No artwork is generated at runtime.
